1. PROJECT GOAL AND AGREED APPROACH
---------------------------------
Build a personal money tracker used on desktop and iPhone through a web app,
then make it installable on the iPhone Home Screen as a PWA.
Track income, spending, cash, bank balances, credit cards, transfers, and bills.
Later add computer vision: photograph a receipt, extract details, review, save.
Use simple English and a professional, responsive interface.
Use small implementation steps: change, check, test, then continue.
Use JavaScript rather than TypeScript. Keep components in separate JSX/CSS folders.
Use free/open-source software and avoid paid AI APIs. Railway hosting is funded
through your existing Hobby subscription; hosting is not inherently zero-cost.

4. ACCOUNT TRACKING — DONE/PARTIAL
--------------------------------
Accounts belong to the authenticated user.
Create accounts, list accounts, edit names and starting balances.
Account types: cash, bank, credit.
Cash and bank use ordinary money-in/money-out balance logic.
Debit card spending belongs to its linked bank account; avoid double-counting it
as both a debit-card balance and a bank balance.
Credit cards have separate credit limits and starting debt.
Credit starting debt is stored as a negative opening balance.
Example: owing 2,000 EGP at setup means opening_balance = -2000.00.
The credit limit is not income or money owned.
Credit debt and available credit are calculated and displayed separately.
Credit card edit form supports name, starting debt and credit limit.
Changing an account to or from credit is blocked by the current update endpoint.
Cash/bank type preservation during edits was tested.
Account field validation and database check constraints were added.
Starting balances do not count as income in date summaries.

Current formula:
balance = opening balance + income - expenses + transfers received - transfers sent
For credit cards:
amount owed = max(-balance, 0)
available credit = credit limit + balance

Still missing: statements, due dates, minimum payments, card refunds/fees/interest,
credit overpayments display, account archiving, and full credit-limit policies.
Current available-credit figure is bookkeeping; actual bank availability may differ.

5. MONEY ENTRIES — DONE/PARTIAL
------------------------------
Create income and expense entries with account, amount, date, category and note.
Edit entries, cancel editing, and delete with confirmation.
Positive amount validation, maximum precision and two decimal places.
Money uses Python Decimal and PostgreSQL Numeric rather than float arithmetic.
Ownership checks restrict access to the user's accounts and entries.
Invalid account checks and deletion/not-found behavior were tested.
History shows dates, amounts, account names and notes.
Filters: account, type, date range and category/note search.
Search is case-insensitive; literal wildcard characters are escaped.
Reversed dates are rejected.
History loads ten entries at a time with Load more.
First-page behavior was tested; loading a real second page is still unverified
because only a few entries were available during that test.

Duplicate-save protection:
Each new save uses a UUID request_id with a database unique constraint.
Same ID and same details returns the existing entry.
Same ID with different details returns 409.
Repeated-click guard and failed-save retry were implemented.
Pending money entry is saved per user in localStorage before sending.
Pending entry restoration after refresh and successful retry were tested.

Important remaining edge case:
The idempotency record is currently the mutable transaction row itself.
Editing it can cause later retries to conflict; deleting it can permit the same
request ID to create another row. Review durable save receipts/tombstones before
claiming protection across edits/deletion or adding transfer deletion.

7. TRANSFERS AND CARD PAYMENTS — DONE/PARTIAL
-------------------------------------------
Separate transfers table, not two income/expense transactions.
Cash/Bank can transfer to another Cash/Bank account or pay a credit card.
Source credit card is currently rejected; cash advances require later support.
Source and destination must differ and belong to the current user.
Currencies must match. Amount must be positive with up to two decimal places.
UUID request ID prevents duplicate transfer saves.
POST /api/transfers and GET /api/transfers implemented.
Transfer history includes account names, date, amount, note and batches of ten.
Transfer form updates balances and history without manual page refresh.
Same-account rejection and different-details/save-ID conflict were tested.
Transfers and card payments were verified not to change income or expenses.

Latest transfer-recovery work:
TransferForm now receives userId and is keyed by user.id in App.
It stores pending transfers under a per-user localStorage key before sending.
It restores the original save ID and details after refresh.
It locks fields during an uncertain save and uses Retry transfer.
Storage failures are surfaced rather than silently sending an unprotected request.
Lint/build passed. User stopped FastAPI, saw failure, restarted, refreshed,
confirmed the pending details remained, and clicked Retry transfer.
FINAL CHECKS STILL AWAITING CONFIRMATION:

Transfer edit/delete/reversal and filtering are not implemented yet.
